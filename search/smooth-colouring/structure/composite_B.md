# Periodic tilings with composite |B|: every prime-power |B| reduces to one cyclic family

Status: written 2026-11-26 (explorer pass 5), not yet /reviewed. All arguments are
[own argument, unreviewed] unless marked otherwise. Brute-force checks: `composite_bruteforce.py`
(output `composite_bruteforce.out`). Searches: `../general/gtile7.rs`, `../general/run7.sh`.

Notation as in `prime_B_reduction.md`: T = T_195 ⊂ Z^44, π = {3, 5, 13}, and a periodic tiling
with period lattice Λ gives

    A ⊕ B = G,  A = φ(T),  φ: Z^44 → G = Z^44/Λ injective on T,  |G| = 195·|B|.   (*)

Tools used: **D** (dilation: gcd(t,|B|) = 1 ⇒ A ⊕ tB = G), **Δ**, **Q** — all as stated and proved
in `prime_B_reduction.md` §1. **Lemma A** of `pass2.md` §2.

## 0. Claims

For p ∈ π and i ≥ 1 let

> **(b_i^p)**: there is a homomorphism ψ: Z^44 → Z_{p^{i+1}} × Z_{195/p}, injective on T, such
> that for every k ∈ Z_{195/p} the fibre ψ(T) ∩ (Z_{p^{i+1}} × {k}) is a coset of
> p^i Z_{p^{i+1}} × {k} (the unique subgroup of order p).

and let (a) be "a multiplicative colouring exists" (ψ: Z^44 → Z_195 bijective on T), excluded by
Forcade–Pollington's computation as reported by CCP Thm 5.1 (quoted in `prime_B_reduction.md` §5).
(b_1^p) is exactly outcome (b) of `prime_B_reduction.md`.

**Theorem 1 (monotone hierarchy).** (a) ⇒ (b_1^p) ⇒ (b_2^p) ⇒ (b_3^p) ⇒ … and each (b_i^p) is
itself a periodic tiling with |B| = p^i.

**Theorem 2 (prime-power reduction).** Suppose (*) holds and the π-part m_π of m = |B| is a power
of a single prime, m_π = p^a (a ≥ 0). Then (a) holds, or a ≥ 1 and (b_i^p) holds for some
1 ≤ i ≤ a. By Theorem 1 (which also gives (a) ⇒ (b_a^p)), **if a ≥ 1 then (b_a^p) holds**.

So, for example, |B| ∈ {9, 25, 27, 169, 2·9, 7·25, …} all reduce to the single search (b_a^p),
and **one NONE for (b_i^p) excludes every periodic tiling with m_π ∈ {p, p², …, p^i}** (m_π = 1 is (a), excluded by CCP/FP90).

*Quantifier.* m = |B| depends on the period lattice Λ. The statement is meant for every Λ, and
it is equivalent to the statement for Λ = Per(L), the full period group: if Λ ⊆ Per(L) then
m(Λ) = m(Per(L))·[Per(L) : Λ], so m_π(Per(L)) divides m_π(Λ). Precisely, given NONE for (b_i^p)
and CCP/FP90: **for every periodic tiling L, the π-part of [Z^44 : Per(L)]/195 is not in
{1, p, …, p^i}**; equivalently, no period lattice of L has index 195·m with m_π ∈ {1, p, …, p^i}.
All "no periodic tiling has m_π ∈ …" statements below are meant in this sense.

**Theorem 3 (finite reformulation of the whole p-power family).** ∃ i ≥ 1 with (b_i^p) ⇔ there
are a partition 𝒫 of T into 195/p blocks of size p, a homomorphism ψ_K: Z^44 → Z_{195/p} constant
on each block and taking distinct values on distinct blocks, and a homomorphism f: M → Z_p,
where M ⊆ Z^44 is the lattice generated by {x − y : x, y in the same block}, with f(x − y) ≠ 0
for all x ≠ y in the same block. (No unbounded group appears.)

  Hence (Theorems 1, 2, plus CCP/FP90) **no periodic tiling has m_π ∈ {3, 9}**; in particular none

**Remaining gap (exact).** Periodic tilings whose index 195·m has m_π divisible by at least two
of 3, 5, 13 (m_π ∈ {15, 39, 45, 65, 75, 117, 195, …}), plus (b_i^p) for the i not yet searched,
plus (as always) non-periodic tilings. §5 records what survives for mixed m_π.

## 1. Two lemmas

**Lemma V (vanishing sums of p prime-power roots of unity).** Let p be prime, e ≥ 1, and let
ξ_1, …, ξ_n ∈ μ_{p^e} (repetitions allowed) with Σ ξ_k = 0. Then the multiset {ξ_k} is a union
of cosets of μ_p, i.e. n = p·Σ_u c_u and for each coset ζμ_p the multiplicity of every element of
ζμ_p is the same number c(ζμ_p). In particular, if n = p, the ξ_k are distinct and form one coset
of μ_p.

*Proof.* Let ζ = e^{2πi/p^e}, ω = ζ^{p^{e−1}}. Write each exponent x ∈ Z/p^e uniquely as
x = u + v·p^{e−1}, 0 ≤ u < p^{e−1}, 0 ≤ v < p; then ζ^x = ζ^u ω^v. Since
[Q(ζ):Q(ω)] = φ(p^e)/φ(p) = p^{e−1} and ζ is a root of y^{p^{e−1}} − ω ∈ Q(ω)[y], this is the
minimal polynomial of ζ over Q(ω), so 1, ζ, …, ζ^{p^{e−1}−1} is a Q(ω)-basis of Q(ζ). Let n_{u,v}
be the number of k with ξ_k = ζ^u ω^v. Then 0 = Σ_u ζ^u (Σ_v n_{u,v} ω^v) forces
Σ_v n_{u,v} ω^v = 0 for each u. The only Q-linear relations among 1, ω, …, ω^{p−1} are the
multiples of 1 + ω + … + ω^{p−1} (the minimal polynomial of ω is Φ_p, of degree p − 1), so
n_{u,v} = c_u is independent of v. If n = p then p·Σ c_u = p, so exactly one c_u is 1. ∎
(For e = 1 the statement is the case u = 0.) This is the classical prime-power case of the
structure of vanishing sums; I did not look up a reference and give the proof instead.

**Lemma C (characters in the spectrum of B are injective on prime-size complements).** Let H be a
finite abelian p-group, C ⊕ B = H with |C| = p, and χ ∈ Ĥ, χ ≠ 1, with B̂(χ) := Σ_{b∈B} χ(b) ≠ 0.
Then χ is injective on C and χ(C) is a coset of μ_p in χ(H). Moreover such χ exists whenever
B ≠ H.

*Proof.* 1_C * 1_B = 1_H, so Ĉ(χ)B̂(χ) = Σ_{h∈H} χ(h) = 0 for χ ≠ 1, hence Ĉ(χ) = 0. The values
χ(c) lie in μ_{p^e} with p^e = exp(H), and Lemma V with n = p gives the claim (χ(H) is cyclic and
contains μ_p, since χ ≠ 1). If B̂(χ) = 0 for all χ ≠ 1, Fourier inversion makes 1_B constant,
equal to |B|/|H| ∉ {0, 1} when ∅ ≠ B ≠ H. ∎

Note that one χ serves *all* complements C of the same B simultaneously. That is the point.

(By-product, not used below: the same computation gives χ(p(c − c')) = 1 for all χ ∈ supp B̂, i.e.
**B + p(c − c') = B** — the finite-group analogue of Horak–Kim Thm 6 (verbatim in `pass2.md` §1).
Checked in `composite_bruteforce.py` as "Lemma P".)

## 2. Proof of Theorem 1

- (a) ⇒ (b_1^p): if ψ: T → Z_195 ≅ Z_p × Z_{195/p} is bijective, each Z_{195/p}-fibre is all of
  Z_p × {k}. Compose with the injective homomorphism ι: Z_p → Z_{p²}, x ↦ px; the image pZ_{p²} is
  the order-p subgroup.
- (b_i^p) ⇒ (b_{i+1}^p): compose with ι: Z_{p^{i+1}} → Z_{p^{i+2}}, x ↦ px (injective
  homomorphism); a coset x + p^i Z_{p^{i+1}} goes to px + p^{i+1} Z_{p^{i+2}}.
- (b_i^p) is a tiling: ψ(T) is a union of 195/p cosets of P = p^iZ_{p^{i+1}} × {0}, one over each
  k, and P ⊕ ({0, …, p^i − 1} × {0}) = Z_{p^{i+1}} × {0}, so ψ(T) ⊕ ({0,…,p^i−1} × {0}) = G with
  |B| = p^i. ∎

## 3. Proof of Theorem 2

1. **Reduce to a π-group.** By Lemma A of `pass2.md` (steps 2–4 of its proof, which use D with the
   roles of the factors swapped, dilating A by some k with gcd(k,195) = 1 and k·G = G_π), we may
   replace G by its Hall π-subgroup G_π, φ by kφ (still a homomorphism injective on T) and B by
   one of its fibres, of size m_π. If m_π = 1 then kφ: T → G_π is a bijection onto a group of
   order 195, which is cyclic: outcome (a). So assume G is a π-group and |B| = p^a, a ≥ 1.
2. **Shape of G.** |G| = 195·p^a, so G = H × K with H the p-Sylow subgroup, |H| = p^{a+1}, and K
   the product of the other two Sylow subgroups, each of prime order, so K ≅ Z_{195/p}.
3. **Push B into H.** t = 195/p is coprime to p^a, so A ⊕ tB = G by D. Multiplication by t kills
   K and maps H into H, so B' := tB ⊆ H, |B'| = p^a. A and φ are unchanged.
4. **Fibres.** For k ∈ K let A_k = {π_H(x) : x ∈ A, π_K(x) = k} ⊆ H. As in
   `prime_B_reduction.md` 2b: every element (h, k) is uniquely a + b' with b' ∈ B' ⊆ H, and then
   π_K(a) = k; so A_k ⊕ B' = H and |A_k| = p for every k.
5. **One character for all fibres.** B' ≠ H (|B'| = p^a < p^{a+1}), so by Lemma C there is
   χ ∈ Ĥ, χ ≠ 1, with B̂'(χ) ≠ 0, and for **every** k, χ is injective on A_k and χ(A_k) is a coset
   of μ_p. Let p^j = ord χ (1 ≤ j ≤ a + 1, since exp H divides p^{a+1}) and identify
   χ(H) = μ_{p^j} with Z_{p^j}; μ_p becomes p^{j−1}Z_{p^j}.
6. **The new homomorphism.** ψ := (χ∘π_H∘φ, π_K∘φ): Z^44 → Z_{p^j} × K is a homomorphism. It is
   injective on T: points of T with different K-coordinate stay different, and within one K-fibre
   χ is injective on A_k (φ is injective on T). Its fibre over k is χ(A_k) × {k}, a coset of the
   order-p subgroup.
   - j = 1: ψ(T) has 195 points in a group of order 195, so ψ is a bijection onto Z_p × K ≅ Z_195:
     outcome (a).
   - j ≥ 2: this is (b_{j−1}^p) with 1 ≤ j − 1 ≤ a. ∎

Remarks.
- The proof does not need Rédei, Lemma 1 of the prime note, Sands' good-group classification or
  Coven–Meyerowitz. It re-proves the prime theorem of `prime_B_reduction.md` in a few lines
  (a = 1: j ∈ {1, 2}), including the cases H ≅ Z_p² (there every χ ≠ 1 has order p, so j = 1).
- Non-cyclic H is no obstacle: even if B' is aperiodic and different fibres A_k are cosets of
  different order-p subgroups (this really happens, see §4: in Z9 × Z3 an aperiodic B' can
  complement both ⟨(3,0)⟩ and ⟨(0,1)⟩), the character χ maps every fibre onto the *same* order-p
  subgroup of a cyclic group.
- Before finding step 5 I proved by hand that for p = 3 an aperiodic B' forces every fibre to be a
  coset of an order-3 subgroup (from Lemma P plus χ(u)χ(v) = 1 for C = {0,u,v} ⊆ H[3]); the brute
  force confirmed it. Step 5 makes this unnecessary.

## 4. Proof of Theorem 3

(⇒) Given (b_i^p) via ψ = (ψ_H, ψ_K), let the blocks be the K-fibres of T. ψ_K is constant on
blocks, hence kills M, and separates blocks. ψ_H maps each within-block difference into the
order-p subgroup P = p^iZ_{p^{i+1}} ≅ Z_p, hence ψ_H(M) ⊆ P; put f = ψ_H|_M. Injectivity of ψ on a
block gives f(x − y) ≠ 0.
(⇐) View Z_p as the order-p subgroup of the Prüfer group Z(p^∞), which is divisible, hence an
injective Z-module; so f extends to a homomorphism F: Z^44 → Z(p^∞). F(Z^44) is finitely generated,
hence cyclic, = Z_{p^j} with j ≥ 1. Then ψ = (F, ψ_K): Z^44 → Z_{p^j} × Z_{195/p}: distinct blocks
have distinct ψ_K; inside a block the F-values differ by nonzero elements of the order-p subgroup,
so the p values are distinct and form one coset of it. This is (a) (j = 1) or (b_{j−1}^p). ∎

Moreover, via the Smith normal form of M ⊆ Z^44 (a basis e_1, …, e_44 of Z^44 and integers
d_r ≥ 0 with M = ⊕ d_r Z e_r; d_r = 0 when rank M < 44), F can be chosen explicitly. Put
c_r := f(d_r e_r) ∈ Z_p. If d_r = 0 then d_r e_r = 0, so c_r = 0, and we set F(e_r) := 0: f
imposes no condition in that direction. If d_r ≠ 0 we need F(e_r) ∈ Z(p^∞) with d_r F(e_r) = c_r;
writing d_r = p^v u with p ∤ u, such an element exists of order dividing p^{v+1}. Since f ≠ 0
(blocks have p ≥ 2 elements), j ≥ 1 and j − 1 ≤ max_{r : d_r ≠ 0} v_p(d_r): **for a given
partition the required level i is bounded by the largest p-adic valuation of a nonzero elementary
divisor of M.** No uniform bound over all partitions is proved.

## 5. Mixed m_π: what survives, and the exact gap

Let m_π have prime set Q ⊆ π with |Q| ≥ 2, r = ∏_{q∈Q} q. As in step 3, dilating by
t = ∏_{q∈π∖Q} q (coprime to m_π) puts B' inside H = ∏_{q∈Q} G_q, with fibres A_k ⊕ B' = H,
|A_k| = r, over K = ∏_{q∉Q} Z_q (K = 0 when Q = π). For each q ∈ Q, the pushforward of B' to the
Sylow factor H_q is not uniform (|H_q| = q·(m_π)_q does not divide m_π), so some χ_q ∈ Ĥ_q ⊂ Ĥ,
χ_q ≠ 1, has B̂'(χ_q) ≠ 0, and by Lemma V (general n) **every fibre's χ_q-image is, as a multiset,
a union of r/q cosets of μ_q** [own argument, unreviewed; not brute-forced]. This is all that
transfers. Theorem 2 fails here because these cosets can repeat (χ_q is not injective on A_k), and
no single prime-power character sees all of A_k. Characters of mixed order give vanishing sums
of r roots of unity of mixed order; for r = 15 these are (by Lam–Leung for two primes, *not read
here, not cited as verified*) unions of five 3-gons or three 5-gons, again with possible repeats.

**Gap, stated exactly:** exclude (*) with G a π-group and |B| = m_π divisible by two or three of
3, 5, 13 (the smallest case is |B| = 15, G = (Z9 or Z3²) × (Z25 or Z5²) × Z13). Ideas for it in
§7.
