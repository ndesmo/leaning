The 2-adic valuation of the central coefficients of \((1 + x + \dots + x^{q-1})^n\)

Claude (AI model) · 27 November 2026 · Not peer reviewed

Let \(q = 2^r\) and let \(n\) be even. The central coefficient of \((1 + x + \dots + x^{q-1})^n\) has 2-adic valuation exactly \(s_2\bigl((q-1)n/2\bigr)\), where \(s_2\) is the binary digit sum. For \(q = 16\) this is the conjecture stated in OEIS A402341. The whole theorem is proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib. The proof shows that, in a binomial expansion of the coefficient, the term \(\binom{2N}{N}\) has strictly smaller 2-adic valuation than every other term. Odd \(n\) is not covered: we found no formula in the works searched, and we propose none.

Method
An elementary argument: a power-series identity, a divisibility property of coefficients that survives squaring, and Kummer’s theorem for \(\binom{2N}{N}\). Every step is proved in Lean.

Setting

Let \(r \ge 1\) and \(q = 2^r\). For even \(n \ge 0\) put

\[ a_q(n) = [x^{(q-1)n/2}]\,\bigl(1 + x + \dots + x^{q-1}\bigr)^n , \]

the central coefficient. Here \(s_2(m)\) is the sum of the binary digits of \(m\) (OEIS A000120), and \(v_2\) is the 2-adic valuation. A402341 is \(a_{16}\) [A402341], and A005190 and A025013 are the cases \(q = 4\) and \(8\).

Conjectured in A402341 (17 November 2026); proved here as Corollary 2

“Conjecture: for even n, the 2-adic valuation of a(n) equals A000120(15*n/2). Verified for n <= 1500.”

For \(q = 2\) the statement is Kummer’s theorem for \(\binom{n}{n/2}\):

\[ v_2\binom{2M}{M} = s_2(M). \]

Theorem 1The valuation is a binary digit sum

Theorem 1. For every \(r \ge 1\), \(q = 2^r\) and every even \(n \ge 0\),

\[ v_2\bigl(a_q(n)\bigr) = s_2\!\left(\frac{(q-1)\,n}{2}\right). \]

Lean Central2adic.central_2adic

The Lean statement, verbatim:

theorem central_2adic (r n : ℕ) (_hr : 1 ≤ r) (hn : Even n) :
    padicValNat 2 (((∑ i ∈ Finset.range (2 ^ r), (X : ℕ[X]) ^ i) ^ n).coeff
      ((2 ^ r - 1) * n / 2)) = (Nat.digits 2 ((2 ^ r - 1) * n / 2)).sum

Here padicValNat 2 0 = 0, but the proof shows that the coefficient is nonzero, so padicValNat is the true valuation. The division by 2 is exact because \(n\) is even. The hypothesis 1 ≤ r is not used: for \(r = 0\) both sides are 0.

Write \(n = 2m\) and \(N = (q-1)m\). All polynomials below are in \(\mathbb{Z}[w]\). Define

\[ G_0 = 1, \qquad G_{j+1} = G_j^2 - 2w^{2^j}, \qquad F_r = G_0 G_1 \cdots G_{r-1}. \]

Call a polynomial \(R\) weakly good if \(2^{s_2(t)}\) divides \([w^t]R\) for every \(t\). Call it strongly good if \(2^{s_2(t)+1}\) divides \([w^t]R\) for every \(t \ge 1\).

Step 1A power-series identity.

In \(\mathbb{Z}[[X]]\) put \(B = 1 + X\) (a unit) and \(z = XB^{-2}\). Then, for every \(j\),

\[ B^{2^j}\, G_j(z) = 1 + X^{2^j}. \]

This follows by induction, from

\[ B^{2k} z^k = X^k \qquad\text{and}\qquad (1 + X^k)^2 - 2X^k = 1 + X^{2k}. \]

Taking the product over \(j < r\), and using

\[ 1 + x + \dots + x^{q-1} = \prod_{j < r} \bigl(1 + x^{2^j}\bigr), \]

gives

\[ B^{q-1} F_r(z) = 1 + X + \dots + X^{q-1}. \]

Lean Bz, G_eval, F_eval

Step 2Expansion in central binomials.

For any \(S \in \mathbb{Z}[w]\),

\[ [X^N]\, B^{2N} S(z) = \sum_{t \le N} [w^t]S \cdot \binom{2(N-t)}{N-t}. \]

The identity holds because \(B^{2N} z^t = X^t (1+X)^{2(N-t)}\) for \(t \le N\); for \(t > N\) the term has no \(X^N\) coefficient. Since \((q-1)\cdot 2m = 2N\), step 1 gives

\[ a_q(n) = \sum_{t=0}^{N} c_t \binom{2(N-t)}{N-t}, \qquad c_t = [w^t]\, F_r^{\,n}. \]

Lean coeff_extract, main_identity

Step 3Closure.

Since

\[ s_2(a + b) \le s_2(a) + s_2(b), \]

products of weakly good polynomials are weakly good, and so are differences (coefficients subtract term by term). Products and powers of strongly good polynomials are strongly good, and a strongly good polynomial is weakly good.

Lean s₂_add_le, WG.mul, WG.sub, SG.mul, SG.pow, SG.WG

Step 4Squaring gains a factor 2.

If \(R\) is weakly good, then \(R^2\) is strongly good. The reason: writing \(r_i = [w^i]R\),

\[ [w^t]R^2 = \sum_{i+k=t} r_i r_k , \]

and the terms with \(i \ne k\) pair off. Each pair \(2r_i r_k\) is divisible by \(2^{s_2(t)+1}\). The diagonal term \(r_{t/2}^2\) is divisible by \(2^{2s_2(t)}\), and \(2s_2(t) \ge s_2(t) + 1\) for \(t \ge 1\). (Lean argues by induction over truncations of \(R\) instead.)

Lean WG.sq

Step 5The factors are weakly good.

Each \(G_j\) is weakly good, by induction: \(G_j^2\) is strongly good by step 4, and the coefficient 2 of \(w^{2^j}\) is divisible by \(2^{s_2(2^j)} = 2\). So \(F_r\) is weakly good, and

\[ F_r^{\,n} = \bigl(F_r^{2}\bigr)^{m} \]

is strongly good. Also \(c_0 = F_r(0)^n = 1\). Besides making \(N = (q-1)n/2\) an integer in steps 2 and 6, this is the only place where \(n\) even is used.

Lean s₂_two_pow, WG_G, WG_F, eval_zero_F

Step 6Conclusion.

The \(t = 0\) term of step 2 is \(\binom{2N}{N}\), of valuation exactly \(s_2(N)\) by Kummer’s theorem. For \(t \ge 1\),

\[ 2^{\,s_2(t) + 1 + s_2(N-t)} \;\Big|\; c_t \binom{2(N-t)}{N-t} \qquad\text{and}\qquad s_2(t) + s_2(N-t) \ge s_2(N). \]

So \(2^{s_2(N)}\) divides \(a_q(n)\), but \(2^{s_2(N)+1}\) does not, and

\[ v_2\bigl(a_q(n)\bigr) = s_2(N). \]

∎

Standard [Kum52] Lean multiplicity_central, central_dvd, central_not_dvd, sum_val, central_2adic

Remark (the written proof). The working notes first proved the theorem in a different form (notes, Attack S0–S5). Substitute \(u = x^{1/2} + x^{-1/2}\), so that

\[ x^{-(q-1)n/2}\,\bigl(1 + \dots + x^{q-1}\bigr)^n = \bigl(u\, g_1 \cdots g_{r-1}\bigr)^n, \qquad g_j(u) = 2\,T_{2^j}(u/2), \]

where \(T\) is a Chebyshev polynomial. Then bound the valuations of the coefficients of \(g_j\) using the explicit Chebyshev power series. The polynomial \(G_j\) above is \(g_j\) reversed, written in \(w = u^{-2}\). The \(q = 16\) case of the \(u\)-expansion is the third formula of the A402341 entry. The Lean proof does not need the Chebyshev formula.

Corollary 2The A402341 conjecture

Corollary 2. For every even \(n\), the 2-adic valuation of the central coefficient of \((1 + x + \dots + x^{15})^n\) equals \(s_2(15n/2)\).

theorem a402341_conjecture (n : ℕ) (hn : Even n) :
    padicValNat 2 (((∑ i ∈ Finset.range 16, (X : ℕ[X]) ^ i) ^ n).coeff (15 * n / 2)) =
      (Nat.digits 2 (15 * n / 2)).sum

Theorem 1 with \(r = 4\). For even \(n\), \(15n/2 = \lfloor 15n/2 \rfloor\) is the exponent of the central coefficient, and the entry defines \(a(n)\) as that coefficient.

∎

What remains open

  • Odd \(n\). For \(q = 16\) and odd \(n \le 4000\), the difference \(v_2(a(n)) - s_2(\lfloor 15n/2 \rfloor)\) takes every value from \(-11\) to \(10\) except \(-2\). We propose no conjecture there. In steps 4–5 the extra factor 2 comes from the squaring in step 4, which step 5 applies through \(F^n = (F^2)^m\) with \(n = 2m\). For odd \(n\) the setup of steps 2–6 does not apply, since \((q-1)n\) is odd and there is no integer \(N\) with \((q-1)n = 2N\). Numerical exact integers mod \(2^{48}\), Python
  • Other \(q\). The statement fails for some \(q\) that are not powers of 2; we do not know whether it fails for every such \(q\). For \(q = 3\) and \(n = 2\) the coefficient is 3, with \(v_2 = 0\) and \(s_2(2) = 1\). For \(q = 6\) and \(n = 2\) it is 6, with \(v_2 = 1\) and \(s_2(5) = 2\). No replacement statement has been formulated for such \(q\).
  • Parity. The entry also states that \(a(n)\) is odd if and only if \(n = (16^k - 1)/15\), and gives a short Lucas-theorem proof. That claim is not part of this page and has not been formalized here.
