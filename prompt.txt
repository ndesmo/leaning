## Background
Conventions: H = -d²/dx² - V with V ≥ 0; negative eigenvalues E_j, counted with multiplicity.

Whole line (d = 1): Σ|E_j|^γ ≤ L_γ ∫_ℝ V^{γ+1/2}, with L_γ finite iff γ ≥ 1/2.
- One-bound-state constant:
  L1(γ) = Γ(γ+1) / (√π Γ(γ+1/2) (γ+1/2)) · ((γ-1/2)/(γ+1/2))^{γ-1/2},
  attained by V = (γ²-1/4) sech²(x).
- Semiclassical constant: Lcl(γ) = Γ(γ+1) / (2√π Γ(γ+3/2)).
- r(γ) = L1/Lcl = 2((γ-1/2)/(γ+1/2))^{γ-1/2} is strictly decreasing on (1/2, ∞), with
  r(3/2) = 1 and r → 2/e. So L1 > Lcl on (1/2, 3/2), L1 < Lcl on (3/2, ∞), and 2·L1 > Lcl always.
- Known: L_γ = Lcl for γ ≥ 3/2 (KdV trace formula at 3/2 [Gardner–Greene–Kruskal–Miura,
  Buslaev–Faddeev, Zakharov–Faddeev; Lieb–Thirring 1976], all γ > 3/2 by Aizenman–Lieb 1978).
  L_{1/2} = 1/2 (Hundertmark–Lieb–Thomas 1998). L_1 = L1(1) = 4/(3√3π) (Read–Schulz,
  arXiv:2609.10478, v2 14 Sep 2026: an unrefereed preprint). Open for γ ∈ (1/2, 1) ∪ (1, 3/2).

Pöschl–Teller: V = ν(ν+1) sech² has bound states E_k = -(ν-k)² for integers 0 ≤ k < ν, and the
k-th eigenfunction has parity (-1)^k. Even ones: ψ_0 = sech^ν and
ψ_2 = sech^{ν-2} · ((2ν-1) tanh² - 1), the latter a bound state iff ν > 2.
With ν = γ - 1/2 we get ν(ν+1) = γ² - 1/4.

## The Neumann half-line problem
H on L²(0,∞) with u'(0) = 0. L^N_γ is the best constant in
Σ|E_j|^γ ≤ L^N_γ ∫_0^∞ V^{γ+1/2}. Write M = ∫_0^∞ V^{γ+1/2}.
(a) L^N_γ ≤ 2 L_γ. Neumann eigenfunctions are the even eigenfunctions of the even extension
    V(|x|) on ℝ, and ∫_ℝ V(|x|)^p dx = 2 ∫_0^∞ V^p.
(b) L^N_γ ≥ 2 L1(γ). The soliton centred at 0, restricted to (0,∞), keeps its even ground state
    and half its mass.
(c) Hence L^N_γ = 2 L1(γ) at γ = 1/2, γ = 3/2, and γ = 1 (the last rests on Read–Schulz).
(d) For γ > 5/2, L^N_γ > 2 L1(γ): with ν = γ - 1/2 > 2, the soliton's even k = 2 state adds
    |E_2|^γ to the sum.
(e) Exner–Laptev–Usman, Comm. Math. Phys. 326 (2014) 531–541, arXiv:1301.4986, Cor. 1.3 and
    eq. (1.6): for scalar V ≥ 0 with Neumann condition and γ ≥ 3/2,
        (1/2)|E_1|^γ + Σ_{n≥2} |E_n|^γ ≤ Lcl(γ) · M.
    Together with |E_1|^γ ≤ 2 L1(γ) M (the one-bound-state bound for the even extension) this
    gives L^N_γ ≤ Lcl(γ) + L1(γ), which beats (a) for γ > 3/2.
(f) Consequence of (e): if Σ|E_n|^γ > 2 L1 M, then the ground-state efficiency
    e = |E_1|^γ / (2 L1 M) exceeds 2 - 1/r(γ). That threshold is 7/8 at γ = 5/2, about 0.924 at
    γ = 2, and tends to 1 as γ ↓ 3/2: any counterexample has a nearly optimal ground state.
(g) Schimmer, arXiv:1912.13264 (J. Spectral Theory): double commutation for Robin conditions at
    γ = 3/2; Neumann is the case σ_0 = 0. He notes (after his Thm 1.3) that removing E_1 by
    single commutation leaves a Dirichlet problem, which cannot give a sharp bound when V has
    more than one eigenvalue.

## The conjecture
L^N_γ = 2 L1(γ) for all 3/2 ≤ γ ≤ 5/2; the endpoint 5/2 is sharp by (d).
- Known at γ = 3/2. On [1/2, 3/2] it would follow from the whole-line conjecture via (a).
- On (3/2, 5/2] the best bounds we know are 2 L1 ≤ L^N ≤ Lcl + L1.

## Task 4 — Certify a counterexample (only if Task 2 finds one)
Turn it into a theorem: L^N_γ > 2 L1(γ) for that γ.
- The Neumann form domain is H¹(0,∞), so trial functions need no boundary condition. By
  min–max the j-th Rayleigh–Ritz value satisfies μ_j ≥ E_j, so
  Σ_{μ_j<0} |μ_j|^γ ≤ Σ|E_j|^γ.
- Use a piecewise-polynomial V and trial functions with rational coefficients, so the Ritz
  matrices are exact rationals. Certify μ_j ≤ q_j (rational) from the inertia of K - q_j B via
  an exact LDLᵀ checked in Lean, and bound M above by a rational.
- The min–max principle for this operator enters as a named hypothesis; everything else is exact.

## Task 5 — Proof attempt (only if Task 2 supports the conjecture)
Start just above γ = 3/2.
- By (f), a counterexample needs e > 2 - 1/r(γ), which tends to 1, so near 3/2 it must be close
  to the half-soliton. Parts of V far from the boundary have ratio at most L_γ = Lcl < 2 L1.
  Aim for L^N_γ = 2 L1(γ) on (3/2, 3/2 + ε), with ε explicit if you can.
- Other routes: Schimmer's double commutation with σ_0 = 0, and the Benguria–Loss / Read–Schulz
  cumulative-mass identity from Task 0 (v = u', Q = q(a), Qp = q'(a), a(x) = ∫_{-∞}^x u²). That
  identity produces the Riccati combination q' + q² only at exponent m = 3, i.e. γ = 1, where
  m = (γ+1/2)/(γ-1/2); on (3/2, 5/2], m ∈ [3/2, 2) (see `riccati_match_iff` and
  `mExp_new_range` in the spec). A mass variable with b' = u^{m-1} restores a Riccati form for
  any m but loses the normalization a ∈ [0, 1].
Write arguments in `NOTES.md` with every gap marked, and formalize self-contained lemmas in Lean.
